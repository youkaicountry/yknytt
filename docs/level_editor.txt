Level editor notes

Look (KS+ skin):
- img/chrome.png is KS+'s editor with the live parts wiped (screen view, diamonds, change bar, palette, minimap,
  numbers), drawn 22px below our menu bar so it lines up with KS+. img/objects.png is the right panel, so greying
  the object picker greys it too. Our controls sit on top, flat or as TextureButtons with KS+'s pressed looks
- img/digits.fnt is KS+'s counter font (white, coloured by the label), 2px apart and drawn over the labels like KS+
- Diamonds (diamond.png, diamond_face.png) are KS+'s outline and face: faces hide the lines behind them, a chosen
  layer fills with its colour and gets a yellow number, a hidden one fades
- Yellow means on, like KS+'s chosen tab letter: SET START while placing, play buttons while playing
- Made from KS+ screenshots under Wine by a script (scratchpad skin/build_skin.py); the counters, palette and
  minimap match KS+ to the pixel
- Not done: the map colour number by the tabs, KS+'s X diamond,
  the smiley and the small button left of the screen (unknown), rounded screen corners, KS+'s smaller status font

World map (EXPAND MAP, Go to > World map, M):
- The whole level over the editor. Drag to pan (any button, or a finger), wheel to zoom at the pointer, two fingers
  pinch to zoom and pan together. WASD or the arrows glide while held (Shift: faster), +/- zoom, Home or Fit for the
  whole level. Fingers are followed in _Input: one comes as the mouse anyway, and on Godot 3 the GUI never hears a
  second finger lift. Android's own pinch gesture is skipped while two fingers are down so it doesn't zoom twice. Click a screen to go there, right-click (long touch) for the
  cell menu of that spot. Esc, M or Close go back
- Up close screens are drawn tile by tile (AreaPainter, the screen view's own drawing). Further out each is a quarter
  size picture made on the CPU a few per frame, nearest the middle first, the map colour until then. Pictures of
  screens edited since are made again when the map opens (a stamp of the screen's bytes)
- Colour by music, ambiance (A top, B below), tilesets (same) or the in-game map colour (MapPanel.areaColor and
  areaVisible, shared with the game; hidden screens crossed out). The legend lists what the screens in view use
- Warps & shifts: arrows where Juni goes. Warps from the edge she leaves by to the far edge of where she lands,
  shifts from the shift to its target spot, flag warps dashed from the screen they're on. Red: nothing there.
  Flag warps that count artifacts aren't drawn
- A screen's shifts of one letter share their settings, so they're one arrow, from the one nearest their middle; hovering
  it shades every cell they cover. The Manor (TigerNDV) fills screens with them: 56629 shifts, 1440 arrows
  Close up (tiles 8px or more) the arrow fans out into one thin arrow per shift: all to one spot when the target is
  absolute, side by side when it's relative
- Screens drawn under 60px link centre to centre (one line per pair, both heads when it goes both ways), and arrows
  shrink to half from afar. Over 300 lines in view they're faint (only before they fan out), and hovering a screen draws
  its own, out and in, in full
- Checkboxes: Warps & shifts, Pickups (powers, keys, creatures, coins, artifacts), Saves (off at first, they're
  everywhere), Signs & triggers (signs, triggers, wins, custom objects). The key lists every kind the level has; a
  click on a line turns that kind on or off (off: hollow swatch, grey words), and a checkbox is on while any of its
  kinds is. Marks are rings round the object up close, dots further out. The start is Juni in a yellow frame
- CheckBox styles in EditorTheme all share one empty style with the default margins: only overriding the pressed
  ones made the words jump 4px whenever a box was ticked (the forms' checkboxes too)
- Hovering a screen tells its values, what's on it and where its warps and shifts go
- Hovering a mark or an arrow: a tooltip right by the pointer (drawn, no delay) and its line in the key lights up
- Clicking a mark or an arrow goes to its screen and opens its form when it has one (objectKey, like the cell menu's
  Edit items): signs, triggers, shifts, warps, custom objects; a flag warp opens its keys in the Warp form.
  Right-clicking one gives the cell menu for its own cell. What's under the pointer is worked out at the click, so taps
  work without hovering
- Hovering a key line: what it means pulses, everything else gets a grey wash or fades. Matches out of view show as
  arrows on the map's edge pointing at them, close ones sharing one arrow with a count, kept off the key
- Speed on huge levels (measured on Azure Serenity Expansion: 13615 screens, 21208 warps and shifts, 45932 marks):
  - Godot 3 draws its own antialiased lines and circles one call each. Arrows, their dots, mark dots and rings, and
    edge arrows go into one triangle mesh per layer instead, with soft edges made of triangles, so they look the same
  - Zoomed out, thousands of the same dot or line land on the same pixels: one per 2px spot and colour is drawn,
    one screen per pixel under a pixel
  - Arrows are indexed by 8x8 screen squares and marks by screen, so a view, a redraw or the pointer only look at
    their own. Lines across much of the level are always looked at
  - The map is drawn when it changes. Pulses, edge arrows, the hovered screen, the key and the tooltip are on the
    Overlay node above it. With more than 1500 things lit their glow is made once per view and only its brightness
    pulses; fewer pulse in size as before
  - Map colours, hidden screens, which kinds a level has and what a lit key line matches are read once. Objects'
    kinds come from a 256x256 table
  - Screens narrower than 6px are their picture's average colour (plain rects are batched, pictures aren't); under a
    pixel no picture is made at all. The map shows new pictures at most every 8 of its own draw times
  - Frames are four filled strips: Godot's outline is lines inset by half their width, which collapse on a screen
    a few pixels big and log "triangulation failed"
  - Azure: whole level map draw 206 -> 5-13 ms, idle 28 -> 11 ms a frame, a lit key line 37-70 -> 12-16 ms,
    zoomed in about 3 ms, opening 1.7 -> 0.9 s
  - Still costs: each screen picture is its own texture (an atlas would batch them), about 36KB each
- Custom object sheets too big to be a texture (Azure has 600x36000 and 600x61440 ones): the editor decodes the file
  once and keeps only the first frame for its preview (EditorAssets.firstFrame), then the game's repack, then the
  plain load. The game itself still logs "Image height ... cannot be greater than 16384" for sheets too big to repack
- Later maybe: problems overlay (solid tiles that don't meet across edges, warps into nothing already show red)

Screen values (KS+ layout):
- Music, A Ambiance, B Ambiance rows and the Background / Tileset tabs are counters: click +1, right-click -1,
  wheel, Shift for 10, 0-255 wrapping. The A and B buttons only choose which tileset the palette shows
- Changes to a screen are undoable. On an empty screen they only set what painting there will make
- The play buttons preview a channel, Play all plays the three. They follow the screen you're on

Script panel (Script > at the right of the menu bar):
- Widens the editor to 1010 like KS+. World.ini as text, at the current screen's section
- The text is the working copy, live both ways: typing in it updates the forms, typing in a form updates it
  (and selects that line). Clicking a form field selects its line too
- The level takes the text when it loses focus (click anywhere else, Esc, Enter in a field), and before save,
  test and undo. Each of those is one undo step. The world reparses World.ini and every screen rereads its section
- Right-click in the text adds to Godot's own menu: Go to xNyM when the line is in another screen's section,
  Edit Sign(A) in its form when a form has a field for that key (IniForm.find matches the field's Key pattern,
  so new forms get it for free)
- Forms below edit single keys. IniForm fills its fields (IniLineEdit, IniOptionButton, IniCheckBox) from INI
  sections: the screen's, the form's Section, or a field's own Section. Sections and keys hold the form's letters:
  Sign{1}({0}) is Sign2(B) while B and page 2 are chosen, Custom Object {1}{0} is Custom Object B5. Letters come
  from IniLetterButtons (a fixed set) or IniLetterEdits (typed: an object number, a cutscene name)
- A checkbox writes its On value or removes the key; On is False for keys that default to True (Visible,
  Effect, Quantize, Trig AbsoluteTarget, Trig AsOne, Map). An option's Values say what its items stand for
- The forms are made by a script (scratchpad forms/gen_forms.py) that writes them into both scenes, so they match.
  Tabs use slim styles in the scene so all eight fit
- Tabs (KS+'s, every key the game reads):
  Sign: A-C, pages 1-5, shift and trigger pages, title, subtitle
  Warp: the four warps, flag warps A B C All
  Shift, Trig: A-C, every key
  Misc: tint, tint clear, tint ink, attach, overlay; shown on the map, shown as, map colour
  Cut: this screen's win scene; any cutscene's music and fade colour; song loops
  World: name, author, description, size, no map, difficulties, categories, character, Juni's clothes and skin,
    sign/title/subtitle fonts, power and coin images, artifact images
  CO: custom objects in bank 255 and 254: image, frame size, offset, the stock object it acts like, animation
    frames, loop, repeat, speed, colour, harmless
- Options > Follow the script cursor: moving the text cursor (click or keys) goes to that screen and shows the
  line's form with its letters. Off: only right-click does that
- Not in the forms: [Custom Character] (only a list KS+ caches), SinglePass and AltDie (YKnytt doesn't read them).
  Fonts: KS+'s notes say Sign/Title/Subtitle=, but GDKnyttGame.loadFont reads font/title/subtitle. The form uses
  KS+'s keys; which is right needs checking against a real level
- KS+ helpers not done: the sign preview box, warp targets shown as screens, dropdowns of the level's cutscene
  and character images, the object buttons under the forms, the map colour by the tabs, the palette colour square
- Scripts later could reuse IniForm for values they declare per screen

Testing:
- TEST LEVEL (F5) plays from the level's start, SET START moves the start (undoable, saved in DefaultSavegame.ini)
- Test > From here (F6) plays from the hovered cell, or from the next click
- Right-click a cell (touch: long press): Pick, Test from here (with the chosen profile), Set start here.
  KS+ picks on right-click straight away; ours asks first so testing and the start are one click away
- On an object with World.ini data the menu also has Edit sign A / title / warp / shift A / trigger A / win scene /
  custom object 5: opens the Script panel at that form and its letters, ready to type. Sign areas open their sign;
  trigger spawn points and delete areas open their trigger
- Esc lets go of a script field (keeping the text), stops placing, then stops painting (same as clicking the
  current layer diamond). Esc never leaves; Backspace and F2 do
- F5 or the pause menu's Back to editor returns. The editor waits off the tree with its undo history, like a cutscene's game does
- The game plays the map the editor holds in memory, unsaved changes included
- While testing nothing is written: no save files, no map cache, no stats sent

Level start (Edit > Level start, right-click SET START):
- DefaultSavegame.ini in the same form as test profiles: start, powers, flags, collectables, coins spent, More.
  Only the keys that change are rewritten (KnyttUtil.setIniValue), so the rest of the file stays byte for byte.
  [World] is left alone (it's the save's folder name)
- Each change is one undo step, typing in More is one step until it loses focus or the dialog closes
- Undo doesn't reach the menu while the dialog is open (it's modal); after an undo it shows the file as it is
- Pick moves the level start like SET START. Test plays what the dialog shows
- Use as level start (in the dialog, or Test > Use X as level start for the chosen profile): the profile laid over
  the level's start, like a test starts, written as one undo step. Picking a profile in the list never writes the level

Test profiles:
- A profile is a start state: a savegame that only holds what it sets, laid over DefaultSavegame.ini at start
- Stored on this machine, never in the level: <data>/Editor/<level folder>/Tests/<name>.ini
- Optional start position (Pick in the dialog), otherwise the level start or the From here cell
- The form edits powers, flags, collectables (bank 19: creatures, coins, artifacts) and coins spent
- Everything else in the file shows as raw INI under More: Character, Attach, Tint, Cutscenes, Endings...
- From last test copies the state Juni had when the last test ended

Scripts later (Lua):
- Anything a script saves into the savegame is settable from a profile already, through More (implicit)
- Explicit: a script declares the values it keeps (name, type, default, range, label), the dialog shows a
  row for each and More stops listing them. TestProfile.inForm is where those keys get claimed
- Custom items and powers: keep them in the savegame too, so profiles, saves and From last test get them free
- Same idea could give scripted objects editor fields (per object instance values in the screen's INI section)

Open:
- Save as gives the level a new folder name, profiles stay under the old one
- Folder (unpacked) levels have no copy in memory, so SET START and World.ini edits go to disk at once
- Detailed map caches from real play may show stale screens during a test
